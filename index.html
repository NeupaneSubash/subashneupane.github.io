Joining The University of Alabama as Assistant Professor

I will be joining The University of Alabama as an Assistant Professor with a joint appointment in the Department of Computer Science and the Capstone College of Nursing. My research lies at the intersection of artificial intelligence, natural language processing, healthcare, and cybersecurity.

I am particularly interested in building trustworthy generative and agentic AI systems that can reason over complex context, ground their outputs in evidence, use tools safely, communicate uncertainty, and support people in high-stakes environments. A central focus of my work is translating these capabilities into patient-centered clinical decision support and responsible healthcare AI.

My broader research spans clinical language models, patient-journey knowledge representation, retrieval and grounding, explainable AI, uncertainty-aware decision support, and security and privacy for intelligent systems.

Research

My research program is organized around a common question: how can we build AI systems that are capable enough to be useful in complex environments, yet grounded, verifiable, secure, and accountable enough to be trusted?

Research Area 01

Trustworthy Agentic AI for Healthcare

From language models to accountable clinical agents

Generative AI is increasingly capable of planning, retrieving information, invoking tools, and taking actions. In healthcare, these capabilities must be constrained by privacy, evidence, clinical workflow, and human oversight. I study architectures and evaluation methods for agentic systems that can assist with bounded clinical tasks while remaining transparent, auditable, and safe.

  • Towards a HIPAA Compliant Agentic AI System in Healthcare Agentic AI · privacy · governance · healthcare systems
  • MedInsight: A Multi-Source Context Augmentation Framework for Generating Patient-Centric Medical Responses Using Large Language Models ACM Transactions on Computing for Healthcare, 2025
  • MedBayes-Lite: Bayesian Uncertainty Quantification for Safe Clinical Decision Support Uncertainty-aware clinical AI
Research Area 02

Clinical NLP & Patient-Centered Language Models

Turning clinical language into useful, grounded patient intelligence

Clinical care generates large volumes of unstructured language. I develop NLP and language-model methods that transform conversations, notes, and distributed clinical context into concise, faithful, and actionable representations for clinicians and patients. This includes clinical summarization, context augmentation, patient communication, and evaluation of generated clinical text.

  • CLINICSUM: Utilizing Language Models for Generating Clinical Summaries from Patient-Doctor Conversations IEEE BigData, 2024
  • Intelligent Clinical Assistant for Personalized Responses and Clinical Summaries AAAI, 2025
  • MedInsight Retrieval and multi-source grounding for patient-centered medical responses
Research Area 03

Patient Journeys, Knowledge Graphs & Grounded Reasoning

Representing longitudinal care so AI can reason over trajectories, not isolated encounters

Many healthcare decisions depend on what happened before, what is happening now, and what should happen next. I study patient-centric knowledge representations and knowledge graphs for modeling encounters, transitions, temporal relationships, and heterogeneous clinical evidence. These representations provide structured grounding for downstream reasoning and generative AI.

  • Patient-Centric Knowledge Graphs: A Survey of Current Methods, Challenges, and Applications Frontiers in Artificial Intelligence, 2024
  • Large Language Models for Engineering Patient Journey Knowledge Graphs AAAI Fall Symposium, 2024
  • A Knowledge Graph for Geriatric Patient Journeys IEEE-EMBS BHI, 2025
Research Area 04

Trustworthy AI, Cybersecurity & Explainability

Understanding how intelligent systems fail — and how to make them more resilient

My earlier and continuing work in cybersecurity informs how I think about trustworthy AI. I study adversarial behavior, explainability, digital-twin security, threat intelligence, privacy, and robust knowledge-driven defense. This perspective is increasingly important as AI systems gain access to tools, sensitive data, and consequential workflows.

  • TwinExplainer: Explaining Predictions of an Automotive Digital Twin IEEE Conference on Artificial Intelligence, 2025
  • LocalIntel: Generating Organizational Threat Intelligence from Global and Local Cyber Knowledge Foundations & Practice of Security, 2024
  • Poison Attacks and Adversarial Prompts Against an Informed University Virtual Assistant ACSAC, 2024
